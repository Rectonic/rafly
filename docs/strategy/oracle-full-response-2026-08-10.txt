The evidence does not yet support “video inventory” as LastBite’s primary commercial wedge. It supports a **paid Store Control Sprint**, with video tested as one possible way to make onboarding faster.

| 1 | Paid Store Control Sprint | Highest | Sell a defined 7–10 day result for the top 100–300 SKUs and ask five owners for a material deposit. |

The fastest sequence is:

1. Pitch five owners using a 60–90 second film plus a before/after Store Control Sprint demo.
2. Require one signed scope and material deposit before expanding product scope.
3. Run two 100-SKU comparisons. Video must reduce total time to a reviewed draft by at least 25% in one store, be no slower in the other, and produce zero silently accepted wrong merges.
4. Test 30 adversarial checkout events before any real integration. Require zero unauthorized movements, zero applied movements for pick-and-replace, median review below 20 seconds, and no “sale detected” claim without trusted POS evidence.
5. Run one or two paid operational pilots while camera research remains separately authorized and isolated.

The safe video chain is:

```text
Authorized capture
→ InventoryObservation
→ identity/count/removal proposal
→ staff or operator review
→ manager approval, correction, or rejection
→ append-only StockMovement, if approved
→ balance and confidence recalculation
→ allocation revalidation
→ offer unchanged or paused for mismatch
```

The best owner-facing demo is a **before/after Store Control Sprint**. Lead with catalog cleanup, expiry action, supplier opportunities, and owner control; show video as one evidence channel among barcode, CSV, OCR, and physical count.

- no-POS verified-offer shop;
- incomplete-POS systematization shop;
- separately labeled camera research lab.

For the priority checkout scene, the honest copy is:

> Possible physical removal — payment unknown. Manager review required.

Only after the manager approves should the demo animate `balance 7 → 6`. If an allocated unit becomes unsupported, pause the offer; do not complete a reservation from camera evidence.

Do not build during the next twelve weeks:

- a custom vision model;
- permanent CCTV infrastructure;
- automatic checkout decrement;
- payment or fiscal inference;
- facial or worker identification;
- a generic POS integration framework;
- autonomous ordering or pricing;
- broad analytics;
- a camera-dependent marketplace.

The stop rule is important: if video does not beat barcode/manual onboarding on total verified time—or checkout review costs more than a three-minute closeout—remove it from the product roadmap rather than training a larger model.

# C. Safe integration of video with the existing trust chain

## 1. Non-negotiable architectural rule

Video may produce:

- an `InventoryObservation`;
- a candidate catalog identity;
- a possible visible-count estimate;
- a possible removal, replacement, or restock event;
- an exception;
- a physical count task;
- a `StockAdjustmentProposal`.

Video may not directly produce:

- a `StockMovement`;
- an offer allocation;
- a public offer;
- a price change;
- a supplier order;
- a reservation outcome;
- a fiscal or marked-goods record;
- an accusation of theft or staff misconduct.

The current source-of-truth rules already say catalog identity is human-approved, OCR is draft data, physical counts are strongest at their timestamp, stock is derived from approved movements, actual payment remains in the existing POS, and camera output is only an observation or count-task input.

## 2. Shared capture-session envelope

Both shelf onboarding and checkout capture should begin with an explicit `CaptureSession`.

| Group | Fields |
|---|---|
| Authorization | `authorization_id`, authorizing owner/manager, allowed purpose, allowed zone, validity, research or operational mode |
| Notice and consent | `notice_version`, staff acknowledgement, customer-notice method, opt-out process, audio enabled/disabled, restricted zones |
| Store context | `store_id`, `zone_id`, shelf/counter location, local timezone, session timestamps |
| Source | `source_kind`, device ID, camera/phone model, lens/angle description, operator ID, fixed or handheld |
| Data handling | Upload mode, store-and-forward flag, encryption status, raw-video retention, evidence-clip retention, deletion status |
| Integrity | Content hash, session nonce, device clock offset, upload receipt, duplicate-session key |
| Model provenance | Detection/OCR/barcode model versions, pipeline version, confidence-calibration version |
| Review requirements | Minimum reviewer role, deadline, expiry behavior, whether partial approval is allowed |

Exact legal consent requirements and retention periods are not established by the sources. They must remain founder decisions subject to local legal review rather than being silently encoded as facts.

## 3. Candidate observation schema

A candidate generated from video should contain:

- `observation_id`
- `capture_session_id`
- purpose: `onboarding_shelf` or `checkout_event`
- evidence timestamps
- shelf or counter zone
- candidate `StoreProduct` IDs
- raw OCR text and barcode, if any
- proposed Uzbek/Russian aliases
- visible count estimate
- count interval, for example 4–6, not just a false point estimate
- event kind:
  - `visible_count`
  - `possible_removal`
  - `possible_replacement`
  - `possible_restock`
  - `unknown_activity`
- proposed quantity or quantity range
- identity confidence
- count or event confidence
- model and pipeline versions
- evidence frame references
- short evidence-clip reference
- occlusion flags
- glare, refrigerator, reflection, or low-light flags
- similar-package ambiguity
- multiple-person ambiguity
- payment status: always unknown unless a trusted external source provides it
- LastBite reservation or allocation proximity flag
- duplicate fingerprint
- recommended next action:
  - review identity;
  - confirm count;
  - physical recount;
  - compare with POS;
  - reject as no change.

## 4. Review and proposal states

Use one common proposal state machine:

```text
capture_authorized
    -> capturing
    -> uploaded
    -> analyzed
        -> rejected_quality
        -> suppressed_duplicate
        -> observation_created
            -> proposal_pending_review
                -> partially_approved
                -> approved
                -> rejected
                -> expired
                -> retracted
```

An approved proposal then follows:

```text
approved_proposal
    -> authorization_rechecked
    -> expected_inventory_version_checked
    -> append_only_movement_created
    -> balance_recalculated
    -> confidence_recalculated
    -> allocations_revalidated
        -> no_offer_effect
        -> offer_pause_required
    -> audit_and_outbox_written
```

Existing beta state machines already separate submitted counts, proposed adjustments, approval or rejection, and application. Terminal states may not silently become active again.

### Partial approval

Partial approval is important for shelf video. A reviewer may:

- approve identity but reject the count;
- approve 8 of 12 products;
- correct a quantity;
- approve a barcode alias but retain an ambiguous package-size question;
- convert an event proposal into a physical count task.

### Retraction

Before movement application, a proposal may be retracted.

After an append-only movement has been applied, do not delete or mutate history. A correction creates:

- a new correction proposal referencing the original movement;
- a new authorized compensating movement;
- an audit link between the two.

## 5. Onboarding shelf-video flow

```text
Owner/manager
    -> authorizes bounded after-hours capture

Operator
    -> starts CaptureSession
    -> films one shelf section at a time

Video pipeline
    -> samples frames
    -> detects packages/barcodes/text
    -> groups repeated views of the same item
    -> proposes SKU candidates and visible-count ranges
    -> flags occlusion and ambiguity
    -> creates InventoryObservations

Operator or staff
    -> reviews identity candidates
    -> scans barcode or photographs package where ambiguous
    -> confirms shelf location
    -> submits reviewed count session

Manager/owner
    -> approves, partially approves, or rejects adjustment proposal

Store Core
    -> creates append-only movement only after approval
    -> derives balance and confidence
    -> rechecks allocations
    -> pauses affected offer if an approved mismatch invalidates allocation
```

### Identity handling

- An exact barcode may preselect an already approved `StoreProduct`.
- A new barcode or packaging variant creates a staged catalog or alias proposal.
- Two visually similar packages must remain separate candidates.
- The model must never merge products merely because their packaging looks similar.
- Raw video text and model-proposed aliases remain traceable.

### Count handling

Visible count is a statement about visible shelf state at a timestamp, not total store on-hand. The UI should say:

> Estimated visible shelf count

not:

> Current stock

Backroom stock, items behind other products, and product in customers’ hands remain outside the observation.

### Offer effect

A shelf observation alone has no public effect.

Only after authorized adjustment and balance recalculation can the system evaluate whether an existing allocation remains supported. A no-POS store continues to rely on physically set-aside units rather than a full-store camera claim.

## 6. Checkout-video flow

```text
Camera
    -> records a bounded shelf or counter zone

Event pipeline
    -> tracks item movement
    -> creates possible-removal / possible-replacement / possible-restock observation
    -> suppresses duplicate tracks
    -> attaches evidence clip
    -> sets payment status to unknown

Staff reviewer
    -> confirms item identity where possible
    -> may mark "needs physical check"

Manager/owner
    -> chooses outcome:
        no inventory change
        physical removal confirmed
        restock confirmed
        damaged/write-off candidate
        match to trusted POS event
        unresolved; count required

Store Core
    -> creates no movement for no-change or unresolved outcomes
    -> creates movement only for an authorized confirmed reason
    -> recalculates balance and allocation
```

### Payment and fiscal separation

A person picking up an item does not prove:

- payment;
- successful fiscal transaction;
- sale finality;
- which price was paid;
- which customer paid;
- whether the item was later returned.

The review card must therefore display:

> Possible physical removal — payment unknown

It must not display:

> Sale detected

A trusted POS record may later be matched to the event, but the camera itself is not the sale source of truth.

### Reservation separation

A camera event involving a LastBite unit must not automatically:

- fulfill the reservation;
- mark the buyer as collected;
- consume the reservation;
- accuse the buyer of taking an unreserved item.

Reservation state remains server-owned and follows the existing pickup workflow. If a manager confirms that an allocated unit is unavailable, the affected offer is paused and the explicit stock-mismatch process is used.

## 7. Reviewer roles

| Action | Minimum role |
|---|---|
| Start authorized capture | Delegated operator, staff, manager, or owner |
| Review candidate identity | Operator or staff |
| Submit proposed count/event | Staff or operator |
| Approve stock-affecting adjustment | Manager or owner |
| Publish or pause offer | Manager or owner |
| Change retention or release footage | Owner or designated privacy administrator |
| Correct applied movement | Manager or owner through compensating proposal |

## 8. What may be auto-accepted

### Maturity level 0 — demo

Nothing model-derived is auto-accepted. Detection can be Wizard-of-Oz.

### Maturity level 1 — isolated research

The system may automatically:

- validate file integrity;
- reject corrupt or unusable footage;
- sample frames;
- decode a barcode;
- prefill an already approved SKU candidate;
- suppress exact duplicate uploads;
- group repeated observations into one draft.

Everything remains a proposal.

### Maturity level 2 — validated operator tool

The system may automatically link a candidate to an already approved store product when:

- the barcode is exact;
- the store mapping is already approved;
- the package size and unit match;
- no conflicting alias exists.

The visible count and any stock-affecting proposal still require review.

### Maturity level 3 — mature evidence layer

The system may automatically close technical no-op cases, such as an exact duplicate or an unusable session, provided this produces no stock change.

No stock-affecting camera proposal should be auto-approved under the current trust contract at any maturity.

## 9. Adversarial and ambiguous cases

| Case | Required behavior |
|---|---|
| Customer picks up and replaces item | Track full window; classify as replacement/no change; no movement; create count task if uncertain |
| Two visually similar packages | Show multiple candidates; require barcode, close-up, or reviewer selection; never silently merge |
| Several items occluded | Use quantity range or `unknown_quantity`; route to physical recount |
| Staff restocks during checkout | Classify as ambiguous restock/removal; do not net unless reviewer confirms both directions |
| Item taken but unpaid | Payment remains unknown; do not call it a sale or fiscal event |
| Camera offline | Queue encrypted capture locally; balances/offers unchanged; upload later with integrity and duplicate checks |
| Model replay or duplicate upload | Use nonce, clip hash, track fingerprint, time/location, and idempotency key; suppress duplicates |
| Reserved LastBite unit removed | Raise allocation exception; do not complete reservation; verify set-aside; confirmed mismatch pauses offer |
| Reviewer approved wrong item | Create correction proposal and compensating movement; retain original audit |
| Bag, reflection, refrigerator-door image | Apply quality/reflection flag; reject or request closer capture |
| Multiple units handled, some returned | Use tracks only as evidence; require final count or manager correction |
| Shelf-to-backroom move | Treat as location-transfer candidate or count task, not negative stock |

## 10. Audit, retention, and public redaction

Maintain:

- capture and authorization audit;
- model version and evidence lineage;
- reviewer identity and decision;
- proposal corrections;
- movement ID and idempotency key;
- balance and confidence recalculation result;
- allocation and offer effect;
- deletion or retention actions.

Use three retention classes:

- raw video;
- minimal evidence clip or frames supporting an unresolved or approved decision;
- non-video audit metadata.

The exact number of retention days is an unresolved legal and product decision. The default should be to retain raw footage for the shortest practical period, preserve only the bounded evidence necessary for audit, and delete rejected or irrelevant footage promptly.

The public marketplace receives none of:

- raw or clipped footage;
- private on-hand balance;
- model confidence;
- device or camera identity;
- staff or customer identity;
- private exception data;
- supplier or cost data.

## 11. Reconciling the attractive demo with honest behavior

The appealing story is:

> “A customer takes an item and stock decrements.”

The honest product story is:

> “A bounded camera sees a possible removal and creates a review card. An authorized manager confirms or corrects the event. Only then does LastBite append an inventory movement, recalculate balance and confidence, check any allocated units, and pause an offer if the approved result creates a mismatch.”

The second story is slightly longer, but it is far more defensible. It demonstrates that LastBite’s differentiator is not raw detection. It is turning uncertain evidence into authorized, auditable retail action.

# D. Cheapest validation before building

## Validation principles

Use three distinct gate families:

- **Business-value gates:** Does an owner pay, provide access, renew, or act differently?
- **Technical gates:** Can the system produce usable candidate evidence under local shelf conditions?
- **Workflow and compliance gates:** Can staff understand, consent to, review, and safely resolve proposals without unauthorized changes?

A technical pass does not authorize a product if owners will not pay. A business-interest pass does not authorize cameras if consent, review, or error handling fails.

## Risks that should make the team stop rather than mitigate

Stop the camera direction when:

- video does not reduce total time to a trustworthy result;
- review creates more work than barcode census, receiving review, or closeout;
- staff cannot participate without feeling monitored;
- owner wants worker identification or automatic shrink accusation;
- footage cannot be secured and kept short-lived;
- camera event can bypass manager authorization;
- false events affect offers or reservations before review;
- custom training or permanent infrastructure is needed before paid demand;
- owners will not pay for the broader operating result;
- underlying catalog, receiving, and staff processes are too unreliable for camera output to have stable meaning.

# Closing conclusions

## 1. Decisive thesis

Uzbek small shops most likely do not need another standalone POS or a magical camera that claims to know inventory. They most likely need done-for-you implementation and ongoing operating discipline: a clean priority catalog, usable barcode and import paths, reviewed receiving, selected expiry control, supplier organization, clear exception tasks, simple owner summaries, and a reliable process for seller-approved offers.

Digital underutilization is therefore more symptom than root cause. The likely root causes are:

- nobody owns setup and data cleanup;
- staff processes do not reliably maintain the system;
- source data is incomplete;
- supplier and expiry work remains manual;
- shop closure and founder-intensive onboarding are too expensive;
- existing POS, fiscal, and marking systems are not used consistently.

Video should play a narrow supporting role:

> A fast way to collect bounded evidence and create review tasks—not a source of stock truth.

That position preserves the built beta’s strongest asset: its explicit chain from uncertain observation to authorized, audited action.

## 4. Investor narrative

LastBite is building a human-in-the-loop retail control layer for under-systemized neighborhood shops. It begins with a paid store systematization sprint that cleans priority products, connects available POS or CSV data, improves receiving and expiry workflows, organizes supplier actions, and turns only verified, seller-approved inventory into nearby offers. AI accelerates catalog cleanup, document extraction, exception detection, and owner decisions. Video is a future evidence channel: it can propose shelf counts or possible removal events, but every stock-affecting action remains authorized, append-only, auditable, and separate from payment or fiscal truth. The ambition is not to pretend that a camera perfectly knows the store; it is to make uncertain store reality progressively more controllable at a cost small retailers can support.

## 5. Red-team assessment

The strongest reason the entire video direction may be wrong is that perception may not be the bottleneck. The sources repeatedly point to setup, staff discipline, receiving, incomplete POS use, supplier processes, and ongoing maintenance. A camera can produce more observations without resolving any of those problems. In dense shops, it may also add occlusion, false candidates, sensitive footage, staff distrust, review queues, installation support, and a dangerous illusion of accuracy. Barcode scanning, CSV cleanup, invoice OCR, a three-minute closeout, and an accountable operator may reach a trustworthy result faster and more cheaply. If the two-store 100-SKU test does not reduce total verified-onboarding time, or the staged checkout test creates more review work than it removes, the correct decision is not to train a better model. It is to remove camera capture from the product roadmap and retain it only as a carefully labeled demo.
